How to Write a CV for Teaching Jobs in Sri Lanka

By The Elegant Grid | Sri Lanka's #1 CV Design Service | May 24, 2026 | 5 min read

Education is one of Sri Lanka's most respected professions, and the competition for teaching positions — especially at leading private schools, international schools, and universities — is fierce. Here's how to write a CV that stands out in the education sector.

Teacher Registration and Qualifications

Include your National Institute of Education (NIE) training certificate or B.Ed. qualification, and any teacher registration with the Ministry of Education, prominently in your CV. For international schools, PGCE or QTS (UK Qualified Teacher Status) is highly valued. For university lecturers, a Master's degree minimum is expected, with a PhD becoming increasingly common for permanent positions.

Teaching Philosophy Statement

Many teaching positions in Sri Lanka, especially at private schools and universities, appreciate a brief teaching philosophy statement in your professional summary. This 2-3 sentence statement conveys your educational values and approach: "I believe in creating student-centred, inclusive classrooms where curiosity is celebrated and every learner's potential is unlocked through differentiated instruction and genuine engagement."

Subject Specialisation and Grade Levels

Be clear about the subjects you're qualified and experienced to teach, and the grade levels (Grade 1-5, O/Level, A/Level, etc.). For primary teachers, highlight your broad subject coverage. For secondary teachers, your depth in your specialisation subjects is most important. For A/Level teachers, your subject knowledge and track record of student results is critical.

Student Outcomes and Achievements

Teaching CVs in Sri Lanka are strengthened enormously by evidence of student outcomes: "Achieved 100% O/Level pass rate in Mathematics across 3 consecutive years" or "Coached debate team to National Finals 2024." Extracurricular leadership — sports coaching, drama club, prefect coordination — also adds significant value.

Technology and Modern Teaching Methods

Post-pandemic, Sri Lankan schools value teachers who are comfortable with e-learning platforms (Google Classroom, Canvas, Moodle), interactive technologies, and blended learning approaches. Mentioning these skills demonstrates that you're a forward-thinking educator.

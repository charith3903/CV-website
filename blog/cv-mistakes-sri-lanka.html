Top 10 CV Mistakes Sri Lankan Job Seekers Make

By The Elegant Grid | Sri Lanka's #1 CV Design Service | May 24, 2026 | 6 min read

After reviewing thousands of CVs from Sri Lankan job seekers across all industries, we've identified the mistakes that consistently hold professionals back. Fix these and your interview rate will improve dramatically.

Mistake 1: A Generic Objective Statement

"Seeking a challenging position where I can utilize my skills and grow professionally" — this tells the employer absolutely nothing. Replace your objective statement with a targeted professional summary that highlights your specific value for that specific role.

Mistake 2: No Quantified Achievements

Lists of responsibilities are boring and forgettable. Sri Lankan job seekers often write "Responsible for managing the sales team" instead of "Managed a 12-person sales team, achieving 140% of quarterly targets in Q2 2025." Numbers make your achievements credible and memorable.

Mistake 3: Poor Formatting and Visual Clutter

Using multiple fonts, random colours, clip art, and uneven spacing screams unprofessionalism. Keep your CV clean, consistent, and visually balanced. The content should do the heavy lifting — not the design gimmicks.

Mistake 4: Spelling and Grammar Errors

Even a single spelling mistake can end your application immediately. Proofread your CV at least three times, use a spell checker, and ask someone else to review it. Pay special attention to email addresses — a typo there means you'll never receive the employer's response.

Mistake 5: Including Irrelevant Information

Listing every school project, every participation certificate, and every minor hobby wastes the employer's time. Every item on your CV should be relevant to the job you're applying for or demonstrate a valued skill.

Mistake 6: Using the Same CV for Every Job

Top candidates in Sri Lanka tailor their CV for each application. Read the job description carefully and adjust your professional summary, skills, and even your experience bullets to align with what that specific employer values most.

Mistake 7: Missing Contact Information

Forgetting to include your phone number, email, or LinkedIn URL is surprisingly common. Double-check that all contact details are correct and working before you submit any application.

Mistake 8: Outdated Information

Sri Lankan employers notice when your CV lists outdated software or skills. Review your CV regularly and remove anything that's no longer relevant or current in your industry.

Mistake 9: Too Long or Too Short

A 5-page CV for a fresh graduate, or a 1-page CV for a director with 15 years of experience — both extremes are problematic. Match your CV length to your career stage and the expectations of your target industry in Sri Lanka.

Mistake 10: No Proofread by a Professional

Cover Letter vs Application Letter in Sri Lanka — What's the Difference?

By The Elegant Grid | Sri Lanka's #1 CV Design Service | May 24, 2026 | 4 min read

In Sri Lanka, both terms are commonly used — but they actually describe documents with different purposes and formats. Understanding the distinction can help you write a more targeted and effective document.

The Application Letter (Traditional)

The application letter is the more traditional Sri Lankan approach, particularly for formal government positions and some established private sector companies. It follows the formal business letter format: your address, date, recipient's address, reference/subject line, and formal language throughout. It typically confirms your interest in a position, states your qualifications, and requests consideration — it's more transactional in nature.

The Cover Letter (Modern Professional)

The cover letter is the modern professional standard used by most private sector companies, multinationals, and international organizations in Sri Lanka. It's more conversational in tone, focused on your specific value for the role, and designed to complement (not duplicate) your CV. It tells the story behind your CV and makes a compelling case for why you are the right candidate.

When to Use Which in Sri Lanka

Government jobs in Sri Lanka: use the formal application letter format. Private sector, corporate, and multinational roles: use the modern cover letter format. Tech companies and startups: a cover letter is standard, and it can be slightly more personality-driven. When in doubt, the cover letter approach is universally accepted across all Sri Lankan contexts.

The Key Differences in Practice

Application letters tend to be more formal, more structured, and more about fulfilling a requirement. Cover letters are more strategic, more persuasive, and more about demonstrating fit. The best career documents blend both — formal enough to be professional, strategic enough to be compelling.

How to Get Your CV Past ATS Systems in Sri Lanka

By The Elegant Grid | Sri Lanka's #1 CV Design Service | May 24, 2026 | 5 min read

You've spent hours crafting your CV, tailored it for the role, and hit submit — only to never hear back. If this sounds familiar, the ATS (Applicant Tracking System) may be the culprit. Here's exactly how to get past it.

Use Simple, Clean Formatting

ATS systems parse your CV as plain text. Anything that disrupts that parsing — tables, text boxes, headers, footers, columns, images, icons, and fancy graphics — can cause your information to be scrambled or lost. Use a single-column layout with standard section headings. Save as a .docx or a text-based PDF (not a scanned image).

Match Keywords Precisely

This is the single most important ATS strategy. Copy the exact phrases from the job description into your CV where they accurately reflect your experience. If the job says "financial analysis," don't write "financial analyses" or "finance analyst work" — write "financial analysis." ATS systems are often literal and case-sensitive.

Use Standard Section Headings

ATS systems are programmed to look for standard section headings. Use: "Work Experience" (not "Professional Journey"), "Education" (not "Academic Background"), "Skills" (not "What I'm Good At"). Creative headings confuse ATS and your information may end up in the wrong bucket or completely ignored.

Include a Keywords Section

Some candidates add a "Core Competencies" or "Key Skills" section that lists 8-12 relevant keywords in a simple bullet or comma-separated format. This ensures the most important terms appear in your CV even if they haven't naturally come up in your experience bullets.

Avoid Common ATS Pitfalls

Don't use abbreviations without spelling them out (e.g., "AML — Anti-Money Laundering"). Don't use the company's logo or images anywhere. Don't put contact information in the header — some ATS systems skip headers entirely. And always include the job title you're applying for somewhere in your professional summary.

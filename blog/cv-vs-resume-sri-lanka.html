CV vs Resume: What's the Difference in Sri Lanka?

By The Elegant Grid | Sri Lanka's #1 CV Design Service | May 24, 2026 | 4 min read

In many countries, "CV" and "resume" are used interchangeably — but they actually mean different things, and understanding the distinction matters when you're applying for jobs in Sri Lanka or internationally.

What is a CV?

CV stands for Curriculum Vitae — Latin for "course of life." A CV is a comprehensive document that details your entire academic and professional history. It typically includes your education, work experience, research publications, presentations, certifications, professional memberships, awards, and references. CVs are common in Sri Lanka, the UK, Australia, and most of Asia and Europe. In Sri Lanka, the term "CV" is used for almost all job applications.

What is a Resume?

A resume is a shorter, targeted document — typically 1-2 pages — that summarizes the most relevant parts of your experience for a specific job. Resumes are most common in the United States and Canada. They are highly tailored, concise, and focused on immediate job relevance rather than comprehensive career history.

What Do Sri Lankan Employers Actually Want?

Sri Lankan employers almost universally use the term "CV." When a Sri Lankan job advertisement says "submit your CV," they typically expect a 2-3 page document covering your full professional history in a structured format. They don't expect a one-page American-style resume, and they don't expect a 10-page academic CV either. The sweet spot for most Sri Lankan professional roles is 2-3 pages.

When to Use a Resume vs CV in Sri Lanka

If you're applying for a job in Sri Lanka or with a Sri Lankan company: use a CV. If you're applying for a job with a US or Canadian company (even from Sri Lanka): use a resume. If you're applying for an academic or research position in Sri Lanka: use a full academic CV which can be longer. When in doubt, 2 well-crafted pages of your most relevant experience will serve you well in most Sri Lankan contexts.

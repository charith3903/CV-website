Common Job Interview Questions in Sri Lanka — With Best Answers

By The Elegant Grid | Sri Lanka's #1 CV Design Service | May 24, 2026 | 7 min read

Sri Lankan employers across all industries tend to ask a predictable set of questions. Preparing strong, tailored answers to these questions gives you a significant competitive advantage.

1. Tell Me About Yourself

This is not an invitation to read your CV aloud. Deliver a 90-second professional narrative: where you are in your career now, what you specialize in, a key achievement, and why you're interested in this specific role. Practice it until it sounds natural, not rehearsed.

2. Why Do You Want to Work at This Company?

Reference specific aspects of the company: 'I've admired [Company]'s focus on digital banking innovation, particularly the recent launch of [specific product]. My 6 years in fintech would allow me to contribute directly to this growth strategy.' Generic answers about 'great opportunities' impress no one.

3. What Are Your Strengths?

Choose 2-3 strengths that are directly relevant to the role and support each one with a brief example. 'I'm exceptionally detail-oriented — as an auditor, this led me to identify a LKR 45M discrepancy that saved our client from a significant tax liability.'

4. What Is Your Greatest Weakness?

Choose a genuine but manageable weakness and always follow with what you're doing to address it: 'I used to struggle with delegating — I wanted to ensure everything was done exactly right. I've actively worked on this through structured delegation frameworks and trusting my team, which has freed me to focus on strategic priorities.'

5. Where Do You See Yourself in 5 Years?

Sri Lankan employers want to hear ambition combined with loyalty: 'I see myself in a senior [functional] leadership role, ideally having grown within [Company]. I want to build deep expertise in this industry and contribute to the company's strategic goals over the long term.'

6. Why Are You Leaving Your Current Job?

Keep it positive and forward-looking: 'I've achieved [something specific] in my current role and I'm ready for a bigger challenge that aligns with [Company]'s scope and ambition.' Never speak negatively about your current employer — it always reflects poorly in Sri Lankan professional culture.

CV Tips for Fresh Graduates in Sri Lanka — Land Your First Job

By The Elegant Grid | Sri Lanka's #1 CV Design Service | May 24, 2026 | 6 min read

Writing your first CV in Sri Lanka as a fresh graduate can feel overwhelming — especially when most job postings seem to require experience you don't have. But the truth is, every professional you admire once had their first day. Here's how to write a CV that stands out even without years of experience.

Lead with a Strong Summary

Your professional summary should position you as a high-potential candidate, not apologize for your lack of experience. For example: "Highly motivated Business Administration graduate from University of Kelaniya, specialising in Finance. Proven analytical skills through academic projects, internships, and leadership of the Finance Students' Association. Eager to contribute to a dynamic financial organization in Sri Lanka." Lead with what you bring — not what you lack.

Make Your Education Section Shine

As a fresh graduate, your education is your primary qualification. List your degree with your GPA (if it's above 3.0/4.0 or a Second Class or better), relevant coursework, academic achievements, and your final year project or dissertation. For A/L students entering the workforce, list your results and stream clearly.

Leverage Internships and Industrial Training

Any industrial training, internship, or attachment period counts as real work experience. Describe your responsibilities and achievements just as you would for a paid job. "Analysed market data for 3 FMCG brands, producing insights that influenced the Q3 marketing strategy" is much stronger than "Helped with marketing tasks."

Showcase Extracurricular Activities and Leadership

Sri Lankan employers value well-rounded graduates. Positions in student unions, sports teams, debate societies, volunteer programmes, or religious organizations demonstrate initiative, teamwork, and leadership. Include the role, organisation, and a brief description of your contribution.

Skills to Highlight as a Fresh Graduate

List relevant technical skills (Microsoft Office, Python, Photoshop, AutoCAD — whatever is relevant to your field), language skills (especially English proficiency level), and soft skills backed by evidence. Don't just list "communication skills" — support it: "Delivered 3 presentations to panels of 20+ academic staff during final year project."

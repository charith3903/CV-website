How to Write a CV for Banking Jobs in Sri Lanka

By The Elegant Grid | Sri Lanka's #1 CV Design Service | May 24, 2026 | 6 min read

Sri Lanka's banking sector — led by institutions like Bank of Ceylon, People's Bank, Commercial Bank, Hatton National Bank, and Sampath Bank — receives hundreds of applications for every vacancy. Your CV needs to be exceptional to even get noticed. Here's the formula that works.

What Sri Lankan Banks Look for in a CV

Banks in Sri Lanka specifically look for: relevant qualifications (IBSL, CASL, ACCA, CFA, MBA), customer service orientation, numerical and analytical ability, integrity and attention to detail, knowledge of banking regulations and compliance, and communication skills in both Sinhala/Tamil and English. Your CV must clearly demonstrate these qualities.

Highlight Your Banking Qualifications First

If you hold IBSL (Institute of Bankers of Sri Lanka) qualifications, CIB, or any banking-specific certification, put them prominently — either in a dedicated certifications section near the top or immediately after your professional summary. Bankers who advance quickly typically hold both academic and professional qualifications.

Structure Your Experience for Banking

For each banking role, highlight: types of accounts/products managed, sales targets achieved (with numbers), KYC/AML compliance experience, customer satisfaction scores, and any leadership or supervisory responsibilities. Banks love data — "Managed a portfolio of 450 SME clients with a total loan book of LKR 180 million" is exactly the kind of detail that wins interviews.

Key Skills to Include on a Banking CV

Include skills such as: Credit Risk Analysis, KYC/AML, Trade Finance, Treasury Management, FINACLE (core banking software), Retail Banking, SME Lending, Relationship Management, Financial Reporting, and Regulatory Compliance. Tailor this list based on the specific banking role you're applying for.

Dress Your CV Like a Banker

Banking CVs in Sri Lanka should be conservative, clean, and professional. Avoid flashy designs. Use a simple, structured format in black and white or with minimal colour. The content must do the work — your CV is your first impression of the professional standards you'd bring to the role.

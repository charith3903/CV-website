How to Write a CV for IT Jobs in Sri Lanka

By The Elegant Grid | Sri Lanka's #1 CV Design Service | May 24, 2026 | 6 min read

Sri Lanka's IT industry has grown into one of the country's most dynamic sectors, with major hubs in Colombo (Rajagiriya, Malabe) and Kandy. Companies like Virtusa, IFS, WSO2, 99X, and ZerynX are constantly seeking skilled IT professionals. Here's how to write a CV that gets you noticed in this competitive field.

Lead with Technical Skills Prominently

For IT CVs in Sri Lanka, a dedicated technical skills section near the top of the document is essential. Organise your skills by category: Programming Languages, Frameworks & Libraries, Databases, Cloud Platforms, DevOps Tools, and Testing. Example: "Python, Java, JavaScript, React, Node.js, Django, PostgreSQL, MySQL, AWS, Docker, Kubernetes, Git, Jenkins."

Showcase Projects — Not Just Job Titles

In IT, what you've built matters more than where you've worked. Include a projects section that describes your key projects — both professional and personal/GitHub. For each project: name, technology stack, your role, and the impact or outcome. "Built a microservices-based e-commerce platform using React and Node.js, serving 50,000 daily users" is exactly what IT hiring managers in Sri Lanka want to see.

Certifications That Matter for Sri Lankan IT Jobs

Highly valued IT certifications in Sri Lanka include: AWS Certified Solutions Architect, Google Cloud Professional, Microsoft Azure Fundamentals, Cisco CCNA, CompTIA Security+, Certified Scrum Master (CSM), PMP, and various Google/Meta professional certificates. Include certification name, issuing body, and year obtained.

GitHub, Portfolio, and LinkedIn

Including your GitHub profile URL and/or portfolio website on your CV is expected for senior tech roles in Sri Lanka. Make sure your GitHub has active, well-documented repositories before you start applying. Recruiters at top Sri Lankan IT companies routinely check GitHub profiles.

ATS Optimization for IT CVs

Tech job listings in Sri Lanka are highly keyword-specific. Use the exact technology names from the job description. "React.js" and "React" may be counted as different keywords by some ATS systems — include both variants if you use both. Avoid abbreviations without also spelling them out at least once.

CV Format for Sri Lanka Employers — 2026 Standards

By The Elegant Grid | Sri Lanka's #1 CV Design Service | May 24, 2026 | 5 min read

The CV format you choose can be the difference between getting a call back and being ignored. Sri Lankan employers have specific preferences that differ from Western markets — here's exactly what works in 2026.

Standard Sri Lankan CV Structure

The preferred CV format for Sri Lankan employers follows this order: (1) Personal Details, (2) Professional Summary/Objective, (3) Work Experience, (4) Education & Qualifications, (5) Skills, (6) Professional Memberships/Certifications, (7) References. This structure is familiar to local HR managers and makes it easy for them to find the information they need quickly.

Length and Page Count

For fresh graduates with under 2 years of experience, keep your CV to 1-2 pages. For mid-career professionals (3-10 years), 2-3 pages is standard in Sri Lanka. Senior executives and academics may go up to 4 pages, but only if the content justifies it. Never pad your CV with irrelevant information just to fill pages.

Font and Visual Design

Use professional, readable fonts: Calibri (11pt), Arial (11pt), or Georgia (11pt) are all excellent choices for Sri Lankan CVs. Stick to black or very dark grey for body text. Headers can use a subtle accent colour if you're in a business role — avoid neon colours. Maintain consistent margins (0.75 to 1 inch) and consistent spacing throughout.

File Format

Always submit your Sri Lankan CV as a PDF unless the job posting specifically requests Word format. PDFs preserve your formatting across all devices and operating systems. Name your file professionally: "FirstName_LastName_CV.pdf" — never "CV_final_v3_ACTUAL.docx."

What Sri Lankan Employers Hate to See

Generic objective statements ("Seeking a challenging position..."), spelling mistakes in Sinhala/Tamil transliterated names, incorrect NIC formatting, photos in unprofessional settings, and references to hobbies that seem irrelevant (e.g., "watching TV") are all red flags that hurt your chances with Sri Lankan recruiters.

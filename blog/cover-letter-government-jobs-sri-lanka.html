Cover Letter for Government Jobs in Sri Lanka — Formal Guide

By The Elegant Grid | Sri Lanka's #1 CV Design Service | May 24, 2026 | 5 min read

Applying for a government position in Sri Lanka demands a different approach to your cover letter than private sector applications. Formality, precision, and adherence to protocol are essential. Here's the complete guide.

The Formal Letter Structure for Government Applications

Government cover letters in Sri Lanka follow a strict formal letter structure. Include your full name and address in the top right corner, the date, and the recipient's full designation and address on the left. Always use "Dear Sir/Madam" or the specific title of the recipient. End with "Yours faithfully" or "Yours obediently" for senior government officials.

The Subject Line Matters

Include a clear subject line referencing the specific vacancy: "Application for the Post of [Job Title] — [Reference Number if applicable] — as advertised in the [Publication/Date]." This helps the receiving department correctly file and process your application.

State Your Qualifications Precisely

Government selection processes in Sri Lanka are criteria-based. State your qualifications clearly and reference how they meet the stated requirements of the post. If the advertisement specifies "Degree in Economics with at least Second Class Lower Division," state your qualification in exactly those terms and confirm you meet the requirement.

Highlight Relevant Public Service Experience

Any previous government service, voluntary work, or community involvement should be highlighted. Government employers in Sri Lanka value understanding of public sector procedures, commitment to public service, and familiarity with government systems and processes.

Language and Tone

Use formal, precise language throughout. Avoid contractions (don't use "I've" — use "I have"). Avoid colloquialisms. Write in full sentences. Keep paragraphs concise. The tone should be respectful and professional throughout — government applications in Sri Lanka are formal documents and should be treated as such.
